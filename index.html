Philippine Politics & Society (2000–2010)

Posted by Admin_Nostalgia · 2010 Decade Review

Politics & Society: A Turbulent Decade

The years from 2000 to 2010 brought dramatic changes to Philippine politics and society—from the end of Joseph Estrada’s presidency to the country’s first nationwide automated elections.

2001 — Estrada leaves office

President Joseph Estrada faced an impeachment trial amid corruption allegations. The trial broke down in late 2000; mass protests and the withdrawal of military and cabinet support preceded his departure from office in January 2001. The Philippine Journal of Public Administration discusses his ouster and the corruption charges.

2004–2008 — Election controversy and impeachment complaints

The May 2004 presidential election was marked by violence that left more than 90 people dead and reports of significant irregularities, according to the Polity IV Country Report. In 2005, the “Hello Garci” wiretapping scandal implicated President Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo in alleged election fraud.

Opposition lawmakers filed multiple impeachment complaints against Arroyo between 2005 and 2008. As documented in SAGE Journals, none prospered, as Arroyo retained effective control of the House of Representatives.

2005–2008 — Defections, coup attempts, and displacement

After the 2005 scandals, cabinet members known as the “Hyatt 10” and former allies including Corazon Aquino withdrew their support. The administration also faced thwarted military coup attempts amid prolonged political instability.

A World Bank survey describes repeated violence in Central Mindanao: the 2000 “All-Out War” and renewed hostilities in 2008 each displaced nearly a million people. About 40 percent of surveyed households experienced displacement during the decade, rising to 82 percent in Maguindanao.

2007 — Senate election protest

After the midterm elections, Atty. Koko Pimentel filed an electoral protest against Migz Zubiri before the Senate Electoral Tribunal, alleging that results had been tampered with.

Across the decade — Rights and land disputes

The International Peace Observers Network report notes continuing concerns about extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances, and landlord resistance to the Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Program (CARP).

2010 — Automated elections and a new administration

The Philippines held its first nationwide automated elections in May 2010, according to an ANC Yearend Special. Benigno Aquino III won the presidency on an anti-corruption platform, following the 2009 death of his mother, former President Corazon Aquino.

August 23, 2010 — Manila hostage crisis

Shortly after Aquino took office, a dismissed police officer took Hong Kong tourists hostage in Manila. The crisis sparked intense debate about police competence and executive crisis management.

Cyber Times Archive - 2000s Decade Review

The Philippines · Decade in Review · Special Edition

The Daily Cyber Times

Vol. X · No. 10 Manila, Philippines 2000–2010 Retrospective
Politics · Economy · Environment Decade Review

The decade's defining story

A Decade Shaken by Scandals—and a Stalled Promise of Growth

Political turmoil and weak institutions weighed on confidence as the country entered a new century.

President Joseph Estrada was impeached and ousted in 2001 following corruption allegations. Gloria Macapagal-Arroyo’s presidency was later marked by repeated graft scandals. Weak governance and institutional gridlock dampened investor confidence and held back the country's growth potential.

Business & Economy

Factories, Exports Feel the Squeeze

Manufacturing's Share Shrinks

Manufacturing’s share of industrial output fell from roughly 36% in the late 1990s to 22.2% by 2010. Local factories faced cheaper imports, bureaucratic red tape, and some of Asia’s highest electricity costs.

Global Crisis Slashes Exports

The 2008–2009 global downturn cut Philippine export revenues by 14.2% and slowed GDP growth from 7.1% in 2007 to 0.9% in 2009. OFW remittances helped avert a technical recession, but roughly two million more Filipinos were pushed into poverty.

Deficits Rise as Infrastructure Lags

Weak tax collection and heavy public spending widened the fiscal deficit to 3.9% of GDP by 2010. Chronic underinvestment in rural infrastructure and agriculture left farmers reliant on costly inputs and vulnerable to food-price shocks.

Environment & Public Safety

Disasters Expose the Cost of Neglect

Payatas Tragedy Spurs Waste Law

Rapid urban growth outpaced waste collection. The July 2000 Payatas landslide in Quezon City killed hundreds and prompted the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (RA 9003), though local enforcement remained uneven.

Forest Loss Leaves Hillsides at Risk

From 2003 to 2010, forest cover fell 4.6%, from 7.2 million to 6.8 million hectares, amid illegal logging, mining, and land conversion. Erosion contributed to deadly floods and mudslides, including the 2004 Quezon landslides and 2006 Guinsaugon tragedy in Southern Leyte.

Ondoy Floods Metro Manila

Typhoon Ondoy (Ketsana) submerged large parts of Metro Manila in 2009, exposing gaps in drainage and disaster preparedness. The Climate Change Act of 2009 sought to integrate climate risks and disaster reduction into national planning.

Polluted Rivers, Strained Coasts

Vehicle growth degraded Metro Manila’s air, while untreated sewage polluted the Pasig River and Manila Bay. The Clean Water Act of 2004 added to efforts to curb pollution, alongside ongoing challenges enforcing the Clean Air Act of 1999. Destructive fishing and coastal development damaged reefs and mangroves; the 2006 Guimaras oil spill devastated fisheries and livelihoods.
